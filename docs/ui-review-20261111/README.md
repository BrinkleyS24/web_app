# Applendium customer UI review — November 11, 2026

Subsequent work: the standalone Ask page's baseline D below is superseded by the [Ask UI implementation review and release](../ask-ui-20261111/README.md). Interview Prep's baseline D is superseded for the reviewed local revision by the [Interview Prep review](../interview-prep-ui-20261111/README.md). This document remains the historical pre-change audit; other page grades are unchanged.

**Overall usability judgment: C.** The shared colors, cards, spacing and navigation are reasonably consistent. The larger weakness is information priority: analysis, disclaimers, evidence and actions often compete for attention. The customer must repeatedly work out what matters. Dashboard and Ask are the clearest examples. Next Actions and Settings already have more useful structure.

These are **heuristic UI/UX grades of observed states**, not measured user-study results, backend correctness grades, or production-readiness certification. This review does not support calling the whole customer experience an A.

**What was actually inspected**

The published `https://applendium.com` frontend at commit `ba197d499ce78c88d6e9bd37fb63ed5e91555a7f`, using the existing authorized customer account. Current assets were `index-FpyeRjnC.js` and `index-BG9DXCfk.css`. Captures used Chromium at **1440 × 900** and **390 × 900**. Public pages were also inspected signed out at both widths; Support was followed on mobile.

The browser used the established simulated extension token bridge and normal Firebase/API authentication. This does not validate the installed extension or Google popup onboarding. Core page responses were live, not substituted fixtures. Telemetry and task-impression writes were intercepted. Source-changing controls were guarded; no application was submitted, task completed, resume saved, preference saved, email sent, or billing operation performed. One preliminary capture pass blocked two read-equivalent POST endpoints too broadly; those captures were replaced after inspecting and allowing their read routes. Private screenshots, body text, scripts and request inventories remain in `.codex_tmp/ui-review-20261111/` at the workspace root. They contain personal data and should not be published. **How grades were assigned**

I evaluated purpose, clarity of the next action, information hierarchy/density, language and cross-page consistency, and feedback/readability on desktop and mobile. A means a clear, low-friction observed main workflow; B has smaller friction; C requires substantial interpretation; D obscures or frustrates the main task; F prevents or materially misleads it. Plus/minus grades express judgment, not a calculated numerical score. An attractive empty page or a passing technical test cannot establish an A for a populated workflow.

**Concrete evidence behind the main findings**

- On mobile, Dashboard's Next Actions heading begins at **y=1272**, after the large hero and Ask. On desktop it begins at **y=815**. This is a problem of priority, not a requirement that every useful page fit into one screen.
- On mobile Jobs, the first role heading begins at **y=741**. The page offers “Check for updates,” “Adjust feed,” and “Recheck applications,” followed by Matches, Needs review, Older, Excluded and Already applied. Those controls represent real different operations, but their presentation makes the customer parse that system before receiving a role. The Excluded count in the tens of thousands visually competes with the handful of useful results. Preserve access to the diagnostic count without giving it equal daily-task emphasis.
- Ask's suggested **“Who hasn't replied in 2 weeks?”** returned **218** applications, followed by **50** source cards. The rendered document measured **11,139px on desktop** and **19,055px on mobile**. Early results were hundreds of days old; follow-on questions appeared at the end. This may answer the literal query, but it places a large sorting and interpretation burden on the customer. A UI revision must preserve complete access and truthful counts rather than silently discard results.
- Resumes says **509 applications have no confirmed saved resume** above the cards. On mobile, “Review submitted resumes” begins at **y=1508**, after the library and zero-outcome table. A role-family comparison changes that count to 205, showing the need to label scope consistently. Both wording comparison and role-outcome comparison worked as read-only interactions; the overloaded presentation is the issue.
- Dashboard displayed **543 tracked applications**, while Settings displayed **509** under “Applications tracked.” The difference is verified on the inspected screens. This review has **not established that either underlying count is mathematically wrong**. The UI does not explain why a customer sees different totals under similar labels.
- The current saved Apply Gate check distinguishes optional missing technologies from core requirements. Older checks are marked for rechecking on the history page. That is useful structure worth retaining. A collapsed history verdict is not equivalent to inspecting every newly generated result, failure or long posting.

**A reproduced broken handoff**

From a freshly loaded Dashboard, click **Prepare for interview**. The destination's `GET /api/suggestions/interview-prep/context` returns **409 `INTERVIEW_SOURCE_CHANGED`**. Clicking its offered **Reload interview source** returns the same response. The failure also appeared in the independent mobile navigation capture.

The selected open task is `prep_interview`, and its URL version matches the current task. It has **no thread, email or application reference**. The source loader requires those invitation references. That makes a producer/destination contract mismatch the supported explanation, rather than evidence that an actual interview changed while the customer clicked.

Relevant code: [source-loader guard](../../../../backend/gmail-job-tracker-be/services/interviewPrepService.js), [context endpoint](../../../../backend/gmail-job-tracker-be/routes/suggestionRoutes.js), [Dashboard](../../src/pages/DashboardNew.tsx), [Interview Prep](../../src/pages/InterviewPrep.tsx). The backend links above cross out of the frontend repository in the shared workspace.

The source guard protects against using invalid evidence; weakening it would be the wrong repair. A safe immediate fallback is to avoid offering this invitation workflow for an unbound general-practice task and give the customer a meaningful review destination. Any general-practice experience needs its own explicit context and must not pretend a live invitation exists. This finding is documented, **not fixed in this audit**.

**Recommended order of work — for review before implementation**

1. **Repair promoted action destinations.** Start with the verified preparation mismatch. A highlighted CTA must be executable with the evidence that produced it. Fix this before polishing its card.
2. **Use Dashboard to establish the shared page hierarchy.** Show the current task and its reason/action first; offer a small daily list and a compact weekly summary. Move the large analysis and embedded Ask out of the path to the first task. Keep Ask easy to open. Retain deep links and show exceptions that change what the user should do.
3. **Contain Ask's output.** Use a concise answer plus an initial set of results with clearly labeled expansion or paging. Offer recent/actionable versus older records as explicit choices. Preserve complete counts, sources and the original question's scope.
4. **Apply the hierarchy to Jobs, Resumes and Apply Gate.** Jobs should lead with roles, Resumes with saved documents and a clear Outcomes view, Apply Gate with the current posting/resume/verdict. Avoid making customers scan prior checks or methodology to find the main action.
5. **Deduplicate guidance and align vocabulary.** Give Dashboard a daily purpose, Next Actions the task list, and Strategy Alerts the pattern analysis. Use the same task/resume/application context across handoffs. Resolve count-scope labels and inconsistent marketing/plan copy.

The preferred approach is an incremental component/content refactor preserving current data contracts and URLs. A complete navigation rewrite could reduce the number of visible tools, but would also make existing customers relearn the app and increase release risk. Prove the shared layout on Dashboard first, then extend it page by page. This does not require adding job sources or loosening qualification rules.

**Risks, mitigations and fallbacks for that work**

| Failure mode | Impact | Mitigation | Graceful fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simplifying hides a degree blocker, unknown resume attribution or unsafe recipient | False confidence or a harmful action | Keep decision-changing warnings visible beside the main action; collapse only supporting detail | Withhold the action or show the unresolved question and source |
| A generic task is sent into an invitation-specific workflow | Repeated error and loss of trust | Validate destination prerequisites when presenting CTAs; regression-test producer-to-consumer handoffs | A truthful review destination, with no invented invitation |
| Paging or summarizing silently drops results | Incomplete or misleading answers | Preserve full count, query scope, ordering and reachable source records | A clearly labeled full-results view, with retry on fetch failure |
| Resume/task context changes during navigation or a slow response arrives late | Advice is shown for the wrong document or task | Retain owner/context identity, revision checks and stale-response protection | Show that the context changed and ask for a current selection |
| Dense panels are simply hidden with no discoverable route back | Important evidence becomes hard to verify | Named disclosures, explicit detail links, keyboard/focus behavior and persistent task identity | Open the original source or established detail view |
| Counts and commercial/privacy promises remain inconsistent | Customers cannot reconcile what the app tells them | Audit labels against their actual scope and one shared explanation of processing/entitlements | Explain unknown/unavailable status without inventing a number or claim |
| Large result sets move into new expensive client rendering or repeated requests | Slow pages and unstable mobile scrolling | Bound initial rendering, reuse existing data/cache and cancel obsolete reads | Keep the summary usable with explicit loading/retry for details |
| A shared layout release regresses another page or a destructive control | Broad customer disruption | Small reviewed changes; existing business/API tests plus desktop/mobile interaction checks | Roll back the frontend build; preserve backend guards and data formats |

No database migration is needed for the proposed hierarchy changes. Do not remove revision checks, immutable resume history, attribution requirements or source validation to make screens appear simpler. Monitoring should record failed handoffs, unavailable detail loads and render/request timing without email text, resume text or question contents. No new telemetry was deployed during this audit.

**What would justify an A afterward**

These are proposed acceptance checks, not results achieved today:

- Several unfamiliar customers can identify the main next step quickly and complete representative tasks without an explanation from the developer. Include a new account, a populated account and a long-history account. A moderated small study would provide evidence this audit and model opinions cannot supply.
- The first mobile Dashboard view contains a useful executable next action. Each page has a clear primary task; optional evidence can be reached and understood. Validate this with real content, long titles and unavailable-source states.
- Every promoted CTA reaches a matching, usable context. A rejected/stale source has an effective recovery or honest alternative rather than a reload loop.
- The 218-result Ask case stays readable initially while all results and sources remain accessible. Customers can distinguish recently actionable records from old silent applications without an unbounded wall of text.
- Customers can distinguish supported qualification, an explicit blocker, missing evidence, an eligibility question and historical interview associations. Cleaner presentation must not merge those meanings.
- Resume wording comparison and outcome comparison are clearly different tasks; unknown submissions remain unknown. Saving, default selection and immutable history must retain their existing protections.
- Desktop and mobile disclosure/navigation tests pass, and keyboard/screen-reader/contrast checks cover the changed controls. Today's twenty baseline captures had no horizontal overflow or client exceptions, and the skip link focused `main-content`; that is useful evidence, not accessibility certification.

**Remaining limits:** this is one account's live web experience plus public states. It is not a four-model grading exercise, a provider-quality benchmark, a real payment test, a study with paying users, or proof of all empty/error/slow-network cases. Those limits prevent a broad A claim. The immediate deliverable is this review and its evidence, ready to guide a focused redesign.
